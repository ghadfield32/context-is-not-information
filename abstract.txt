Context Is Not Information: Testing Daily and Transition Signals in NBA Player Forecasting

Introduction. Player-value systems often add rest, venue, team, and transition context to player-history features under the assumption that more context improves forecasts. We test that assumption in two retrospective NBA forecasting tasks: next-appearance production and post-trade conditional minutes. The question is not whether context is available, but whether it adds predictive information beyond player history.

Methods. For daily production, we predict a fixed box-score production proxy using a lagged ten-appearance mean, prior-season mean, home venue, and log elapsed days. Coefficients are fit on 2016-17 through 2022-23 and evaluated on 2023-24 through 2025-26; 68,546 appearances have complete history, 86.38% of 79,358 observed records, with exclusions driven by missing prior-season history. Paired uncertainty uses 5,000 seven-day calendar-block resamples. For player transitions, we evaluate 313 in-season trade episodes with positive-minute destination outcomes using expanding earlier-only issuance folds across seven annual cohorts, 248 players and 49 issuance dates. We compare recent minutes, prior-season minutes, a history-only ridge model, and a history-plus-destination-context ridge model, all on the same episodes with fixed regularization and training-only scaling. Transition uncertainty is clustered by player and, as a sensitivity, by issuance date. These are retrospective evaluations; historical source availability and prospective issuance are not established.

Results. In the daily task, context lowers MAE from 5.1311 to 5.1158, a 0.30% reduction; the paired difference is -0.01529 (95% block-bootstrap interval -0.02138 to -0.01070), with improvement in all three evaluation seasons. Rolling-ten history alone yields 5.1625, elapsed-days alone 5.1163, and home-only 5.1308. The effect reverses for consecutive-day appearances: MAE rises from 5.3137 to 5.3312 across 10,259 records. In the transition task, history-only ridge achieves MSE 41.56 versus 49.89 for history plus destination context. The context-minus-history difference is +8.33 (player-cluster 95% interval 3.12 to 14.21); an issuance-date-cluster sensitivity is wider (-0.07 to 20.78), so the dependence choice matters. Context improves 148 episodes and worsens 165, with 96.45% of net excess squared error concentrated in the earliest issuance fold, which trains the larger model on only 58 episodes. History-only ridge also improves on recent-minute (-13.93) and prior-season (-16.33) controls. All four recent-minute bands show worse context MSE.

Conclusion. Context is not automatically information. Simple daily context adds a small average gain in routine appearance forecasting but fails for a clear short-rest cohort, while a richer destination-context model worsens conditional-minutes forecasts during player transitions. Teams should require context features to earn inclusion through past-only, cohort-aware evaluation rather than assuming that more state improves decisions. These studies do not establish causal rest effects, participation forecasts, trade profitability, or financial benefit, and the small-sample instability is localized rather than explained.
